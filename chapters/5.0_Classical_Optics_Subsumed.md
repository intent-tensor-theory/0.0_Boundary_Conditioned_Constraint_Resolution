### 5.0.1 The Relationship to Classical Theory

This framework does **not** contradict classical optics.

It **subsumes** classical optics.

When the boundary B satisfies certain conditions, R_B reduces to the familiar laws of geometric and wave optics.

This is exactly how a deeper framework should behave.

---

### 5.0.2 Conditions for Classical Reduction

Classical optics emerges when B is:

1. **Static** (not time-varying)
2. **Planar** (flat surface)
3. **Isotropic** (same properties in all directions)
4. **Homogeneous** (same properties at all points)

Under these conditions:
```
R_B → classical reflection/refraction operators
```

---

### 5.0.3 Deriving Snell's Law

For a planar interface between two media:

```
Let B be the interface
Let n₁, n₂ be refractive indices
Let θ₁ be angle of incidence
Let θ₂ be angle of refraction
```

The admissibility condition on B requires:
```
Phase matching at the boundary
Continuity of tangential components
```

Solving R_B under these constraints yields:
```
n₁ sin(θ₁) = n₂ sin(θ₂)
```

**This is Snell's Law.**

It emerges from the resolution operator, not as a separate principle.

---

### 5.0.4 Deriving Fresnel Equations

For intensity distribution:

```
Let E be the electromagnetic field
Let B be the boundary with refractive index discontinuity
```

The resolution R_B must satisfy:
```
Continuity of tangential E
Continuity of tangential H
```

Solving yields the Fresnel coefficients:
```
r_s = (n₁ cos θ₁ - n₂ cos θ₂) / (n₁ cos θ₁ + n₂ cos θ₂)
r_p = (n₂ cos θ₁ - n₁ cos θ₂) / (n₂ cos θ₁ + n₁ cos θ₂)
```

**These are the Fresnel equations.**

Again, emergent from R_B, not separate axioms.

### 5.0.5 Why This Matters

Classical optics **presupposes** that identity is preserved across reflection.

It tells you the angles. It tells you the intensities.

It does **not** tell you why the reflected tree is recognizably the same tree.

The BCCR framework provides the deeper explanation:
- Identity preservation comes from topological invariance of Φ
- Classical laws come from R_B under special boundary conditions
- Both emerge from the same formalism
