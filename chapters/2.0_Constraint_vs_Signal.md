# 章二・零 (Chapter 2.0)

## Constraint Fields vs Signal Fields

---

**「光は運ぶ。制約は課す。」**
*Light carries. Constraint imposes.*

---

### 2.0.1 The Standard Picture

In classical optics, reflection is described as:

1. Light leaves the object
2. Light travels to the surface
3. Light bounces according to boundary laws
4. Light reaches the observer
5. Observer perceives "image"

This is a **signal transmission** model.

Something (light) **carries** information from object to observer.

---

### 2.0.2 Problems With the Signal Model

The signal model works for static mirrors.

It fails to explain dynamic liquid surfaces:

**Problem 1: No Storage**

If the signal is transmitted, where is it stored in the moving water?

The water molecules are in constant motion.
There is no stable substrate to hold the "image."

**Problem 2: Instantaneous Update**

When the object moves, the reflection updates instantly (within light-travel time).

But the water surface is also moving independently.

How does the surface "know" to preserve identity under simultaneous perturbation?

**Problem 3: Selective Preservation**

Why do topological invariants survive while geometric properties do not?

A pure signal model cannot explain selective preservation.

---

### 2.0.3 A Different Kind of Field

We introduce a distinction:

**Signal Field**
- Propagates
- Carries amplitude and phase
- Example: electromagnetic wave
- Defined by what it **transports**

**Constraint Field**
- Does not propagate in the signal sense
- Imposes relational structure
- Defined by what it **constrains**
- Symbol: **Φ**

---

### 2.0.4 Formal Definition of Constraint Field

Let Φ be a constraint field generated by a stable physical configuration.

**Definition:**

> A constraint field Φ encodes the relational structure imposed by an object, independent of any particular resolution or observation.

Φ is characterized by:

```
1. It exists whether or not it is observed
2. It is defined relationally, not geometrically
3. It constrains what can appear, not what does appear
4. It is generated by structure, not emitted as signal
```

---

### 2.0.5 What Φ Is NOT

Φ is **not** the electromagnetic field (though EM may carry information about Φ).

Φ is **not** a representation (it does not "stand for" the object).

Φ is **not** semantic (it has no meaning, only structure).

Φ is **not** observer-dependent (it exists prior to any observation).

---

### 2.0.6 The Relationship Between Φ and Light

Light interacts with Φ.

Light is the medium through which Φ becomes visible to observers.

But Φ is not identical to the light.

```
Φ = constraint structure imposed by object
E = electromagnetic field
Ψ = what observer sees

The relationship:
  E interacts with Φ at boundaries
  Ψ emerges from this interaction
  But Φ ≠ E and Φ ≠ Ψ
```

---

### 2.0.7 Why This Distinction Matters

If reflection is signal transmission:
- You need to explain storage
- You need to explain update synchronization
- You need to explain selective preservation

If reflection is constraint resolution:
- No storage needed (constraints are imposed, not stored)
- Synchronization is automatic (same Φ everywhere)
- Selective preservation follows from topological invariance of Φ

---

### 2.0.8 The Object Does Not "Send" Its Image

The tree does not "send" a signal to the water.

The tree **imposes** a constraint field Φ by its existence.

The water surface does not "receive" and "store" the signal.

The water surface **resolves** Φ according to its boundary conditions.

The reflected appearance **emerges** from this resolution.

```
Object → imposes Φ
Surface → boundary B
Reflection = R_B[Φ]

Nothing is transmitted in the naive sense.
Everything is resolved in place.
```

---

### 2.0.9 Mathematical Summary

| Concept | Signal Model | Constraint Model |
|---------|--------------|------------------|
| What propagates | Light carrying "image" | Nothing (Φ is imposed) |
| Storage | Required | Not required |
| Synchronization | Problematic | Automatic |
| Identity preservation | Unexplained | Topological invariance |
| Mathematical object | E(x,t) | Φ (relational structure) |

---

### 2.0.10 Transition

We have established:

- Constraint field Φ (what the object imposes)
- Distinction from signal field
- Why this resolves the storage problem

Now we must define:

- Admissibility boundary B
- Resolution operator R_B
- How appearance emerges from R_B[Φ]

---

**「光は媒介。制約は本質。」**
*Light is medium. Constraint is essence.*
