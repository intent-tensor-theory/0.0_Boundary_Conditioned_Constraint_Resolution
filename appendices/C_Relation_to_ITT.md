### C.3 Collapse and Resolution

In ITT, **collapse** is the primitive operation:
> Selection among admissible configurations under constraint.

In BCCR, **resolution** is exactly this:
> R_B[Φ] selects the appearance Ψ that satisfies both Φ and B.

The reflection is not created—it is **collapsed into** from the space of possibilities.

---

### C.4 Constraint Fields in ITT

ITT posits that structure is imposed, not transmitted.

A stable configuration (object) imposes constraints on what can occur in its vicinity. This is Φ.

Φ is not "information about" the object.
Φ is the **structural consequence** of the object existing.

This is why no storage is needed: Φ is always present where the object's influence extends.

---

### C.5 Admissibility and Boundaries

ITT treats admissibility as fundamental:
> What is allowed to happen depends on constraints.

B in BCCR is an admissibility surface:
> The boundary determines which resolutions of Φ are permitted.

Different B → different permitted resolutions → different Ψ.

### C.6 Why ITT Was Needed

Classical optics could not answer:
> Why does identity persist?

Because classical optics assumes:
- Signals carry information
- Boundaries reflect signals
- Identity corresponds "somehow"

ITT provides the deeper layer:
- Structure constrains (not signals)
- Boundaries select (not reflect)
- Identity is topological (not geometric)
