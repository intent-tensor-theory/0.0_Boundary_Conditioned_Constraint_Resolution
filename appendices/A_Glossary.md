# 付録A (Appendix A)

## Glossary of Terms

**Boundary-Conditioned Constraint Resolution**

---

### Core Terms

**Admissibility Boundary (B)**
A surface or interface that defines which resolutions are permitted. The boundary imposes conditions on how constraint fields can resolve. Examples: water surface, mirror, glass sphere.

**Boundary-Conditioned Constraint Resolution (BCCR)**
The framework presented in this work. The thesis that reflection and related phenomena are best understood as the resolution of constraint fields through admissibility boundaries, rather than as signal transmission or image copying.

**Constraint Field (Φ)**
A field that encodes the relational structure imposed by a stable physical configuration. Unlike signal fields, constraint fields are defined by what they constrain, not what they carry. Φ exists independent of any particular resolution.

**Dynamic Boundary (B(t))**
A time-varying admissibility boundary, such as a moving water surface. Despite continuous variation, identity can be preserved if perturbations remain below critical threshold.

**Identity**
In this framework: the topological invariants of a constraint field Φ. Identity persists when these invariants are preserved across resolution, even if geometric properties vary.

**Resolution (R_B[Φ])**
The process by which a constraint field Φ produces an appearance Ψ through boundary conditions B. Resolution is computed at each instant; nothing is stored or transmitted.

**Resolution Operator (R_B)**
The mathematical operator that maps constraint field Φ to resolved appearance Ψ given boundary B. Central mathematical object of BCCR.

**Resolved Appearance (Ψ)**
The output of the resolution operator. What is actually seen. Ψ = R_B[Φ].

**Signal Field**
A field that propagates and carries information (e.g., electromagnetic wave). Distinguished from constraint field. Light is a signal field that interacts with Φ at boundaries.

**Threshold (ε_critical)**
The critical magnitude of boundary perturbation above which topological invariants are no longer preserved and identity fails. Below threshold: distortion. Above threshold: chaos.

**Topological Invariance**
The preservation of properties like adjacency, continuity, and ordering even under continuous geometric deformation. The key to identity persistence in BCCR.

---

### Related Terms

**Classical Optics**
The traditional treatment of reflection and refraction via ray tracing (geometric optics) or wave equations (wave optics). Subsumed by BCCR as special cases.

**Fresnel Equations**
Equations describing the intensity of reflected and refracted light at an interface. Derived from R_B under static, planar, isotropic boundary conditions.

**Lensball**
A glass sphere used in photography. Demonstrates BCCR with spherical boundary B.

**Snell's Law**
n₁ sin θ₁ = n₂ sin θ₂. Describes refraction angle. Derived from R_B under appropriate boundary conditions.
